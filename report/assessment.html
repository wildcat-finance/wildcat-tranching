★ Bottom line

The number

An in-house MVP covering the common ground of the three (two share classes over a Wildcat market, a yield waterfall with senior priority + floor and junior residual + risk premium, a junior-first loss waterfall, per-tranche NAV, a coverage ratio, async redemptions through Wildcat’s withdrawal queue, plus a factory + roles) is roughly 4–6 engineer-months of build + a ~$80–150k external audit, ≈ 3.5–5 months calendar with two strong Solidity engineers.

Reaching full feature parity with Strata/Royco (adaptive yield-split curves, depeg/valuation handling, isolated liquid-junior/illiquid-senior routing, fixed-term recovery state machine, recursive tranching/Pendle) adds another ~3–5 months, most of which is not needed for the Wintermute facility.

The novel, security-critical logic is small: ~600–2,000 lines in every one of these protocols. The cost is not line count. It is (a) getting the credit-loss waterfall right (Wildcat’s risk is lumpy default risk, not smooth mark-to-market, and none of the three handle this exact case), (b) wiring redemptions through Wildcat’s semi-liquid batched withdrawal queue (no reusable template exists), and (c) testing + audit to your existing bar.

The fastest path to the same outcome with zero engineering is the Strata partnership already on the table. Building in-house buys control, no revenue share, and tighter UX, at the cost of the quarter-plus above and owning the audit and risk surface permanently.

2 The common ground (the intersection to rebuild)

Strata and Royco converge on the same eight primitives. This is the spec for an in-house “covers what they do” MVP:

  1. Two share classes (senior + junior) over a single yield-bearing underlying (an ERC4626).
  2. Yield waterfall. Senior gets priority + a floor/target rate; junior gets the residual; senior pays junior a risk premium (equivalently, junior funds any senior shortfall).
  3. Loss waterfall, with junior as first-loss: losses cascade junior → (reserve) → senior.
  4. Per-tranche NAV accounting, marked off the underlying’s value and re-synced on every interaction.
  5. Subordination / coverage ratio. Caps senior leverage over junior and gates deposits/withdrawals near the floor.
  6. Async / semi-liquid redemption (cooldown silos or a request/execute queue), because the underlying isn’t instantly liquid.
  7. Governance: who sets the senior rate/benchmark, pauses, manages the reserve, handles shortfall/default; timelocks + guardians.
  8. A factory to deploy a tranche set per underlying.

Everything beyond this list is differentiation, not common ground: Royco’s fixed-term recovery state machine, IL recovery, self-liquidation, recursive tranching, Pendle; Strata’s three accounting modes, daily-loss floor, depeg/valuation oracle, isolated liquidity routing, and its ~9 per-protocol strategy adapters.

3 What Wildcat already has (and where tranching attaches)

You are not starting from zero, and tranching is not a protocol fork: it’s an app-layer vault stack on top of an unchanged market.

Reuse directly — this is the leg-up

  • Wildcat4626Wrapper.sol (the deployed v-wmtUSDC) already solves the two hardest integration problems: rebasing→non-rebasing conversion via scaleFactor, and pass-through sanctions, with scaled-delta verification. A tranche vault is “this wrapper, but with two share classes and a waterfall.” ~80% of one tranche’s plumbing.
  • scaleFactor() as an accrual oracle. Interest accrues monotonically, so splitting yield is straightforward. This is easier than the three reference protocols, which must diff a noisy ERC4626 share price.
  • Math + infra: MathUtils (ray/bip), SafeCastLib, FIFOQueue, ReentrancyGuard, Solady ERC20/ERC4626/permit bases.
  • The permissionless, arch-gated, one-per-market factory pattern (Wildcat4626WrapperFactory): copy it for a TrancheVaultFactory.
  • The sentinel integration pattern for re-enforcing sanctions at the vault layer.
  • Engineering rigor already in place: invariant tests, fuzzing, the a16z ERC4626 suite, external audits. A new component is expected to clear the same bar.

Architectural seam: tranching cannot live in the hooks system. Hooks are reactive/restrictive (they can revert, and only tweak APR/reserve); they cannot route funds or hold dual-class accounting. The tranche logic must be a separate vault that holds the market token (or v-wmtUSDC) and issues senior/junior shares. Hooks can assist (e.g. restrict a market so only the tranche vault may deposit), but that’s optional.

4 The Wildcat-specific hard parts (where the months go)

These are the three areas with the least reusable code and the most design / risk.

  1. CRUX  Credit-loss vs mark-to-market loss. Strata/Royco/Pareto tranche liquid yield where loss = a falling ERC4626 share price, detected automatically. Wildcat is undercollateralized private credit: the upside (interest) is smooth and easy to tranche, but “loss” is lumpy and discrete. It splits into delinquency (penalty APR, delayed withdrawals, principal intact) versus default or closure with a shortfall (principal impaired). You must define the loss trigger (When is the loan impaired? Who declares it? How is a delayed/partial recovery socialized back?) before any junior-first write-down can be coded. This is a design and likely legal question.
  2. Redemptions through a semi-liquid, batched, pro-rata, time-delayed queue. Wildcat withdrawals are queued into expiry-dated batches, paid as liquidity allows, with a FIFO unpaid-batch queue and pro-rata fills. A tranche vault must queue → wait for expiry → execute, then allocate the partial, delayed proceeds across senior/junior per the waterfall (senior first). None of the three handle anything this complex on exit; they assume instant or simple-cooldown ERC4626 liquidity. This is the largest piece of net-new design.
  3. Per-user KYC / sanctions at the vault layer. When lenders are pooled into a tranche vault, the market only sees the vault as the lender, so per-user credential and sanctions visibility is lost at the market level. The existing 4626 wrapper shows the pattern (re-check the sentinel on every entry and on share transfers); a permissioned senior/junior product re-enforces per-user gating in the vault too.

6 Build vs. partner

Partner (Strata, as proposed)

Tranches live in weeks, with ~zero Wildcat eng. Strata brings NAV accounting, programmable waterfalls, yield distribution, enforced mint/redeem; you bring market + joint UI. Cost: revenue share, external trust surface, less control over UX/roadmap.

Build in-house

Full control, no revenue share, native UX, reusable across every market via a factory. Cost: the quarter-plus opposite, owning the audit + ongoing maintenance, and owning the credit-loss-model risk.

Pragmatic middle

Partner to ship the Wintermute facility and learn real demand/UX, while running the cheap design phase in parallel. It de-risks the whole build; the decision on the MVP follows once the loss model and redemption design are settled.
