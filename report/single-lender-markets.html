Wildcat tranching · feasibility

Senior over the facility, or senior over a sliver?

A tranche set on an ordinary Wildcat market does not make the senior claim senior over the facility. It makes it senior over the vehicle's own share of a pool it competes in. Whether a hooks template can close that, what the proposal to do so gets right, and the two places it does not reach.

1. What pari passu does to a tranche §3

Wildcat pays lenders strictly in proportion to their share of a withdrawal batch. The only ordering it recognises is temporal, between batches. Subordination is internal to the tranche controller, so the market's pro-rata split happens first and the waterfall allocates only what arrived.

2. Three write paths, and the one the proposal guards §5 · §6.2

V2 has no lender whitelist. It emulates one: every hooks instance registers the borrower as a credential provider for itself with a non-expiring time-to-live, so "approving a lender" is grantRole from the borrower's own provider slot. That is one of three ways an address acquires a claim.

V1 had thisV2 equivalent

That last pair of rows is the whole difference. "Is there exactly one whitelisted lender?" was one call in V1. In V2 there is nothing to count, which is why the fix is to carry the list rather than to interrogate the credential machinery.

3. Why deregistering the old template does not close the door §7.3

A hooks instance is a durable market factory, not a per-market artifact. Disabling a template stops new instances being minted from it. Market deployment never reads the enabled flag at all.

4. The bundled deployment §9.3

One market, its controller, and no route for a second lender, as a single borrower transaction. Exclusivity arms at creation, which is what closes the ordering gap: approve five lenders first and a rule that waits for a whitelist of exactly one never fires.

01
Off-chain. Compute the controller's future address from the tranche factory and a chosen salt. CREATE2 breaks the circularity, since the address depends on the factory and salt rather than on the market.
02
deployMarketAndHooks against the new template, declaring exclusivity and listing the predicted address as sole lender. The market now exists and is inert: the only address that may ever deposit does not exist yet.
03
deployTranches(salt, …) deploys the controller to exactly that address and asserts provenance before returning, refusing to create a set whose market does not already list it as sole lender.

Both calls carry static calldata, so a Safe batches them. One constraint shapes the bundle: deployMarketAndHooks takes msg.sender as the instance's permanent borrower and the creation hook asserts the deployer is that borrower, so a third-party helper contract cannot do this on the borrower's behalf. Whoever calls becomes the borrower.

5. The trade exclusivity forces on custody §9.4 · open

The tightest rule is to disable transfers outright, which makes the sole depositor provably the only holder for the market's life. That is incompatible with the ERC-4626 wrapper the tranche layer's whole valuation core is written against, and the incompatibility is enforced in shipped code.

6. What the proposal gets right, and what to change §7

Holds
Hooks are the right seam. They can veto a market action but not alter one, and exclusivity only ever vetoes. A template's requiredFlags are forced on regardless of what the borrower requests, so the rule cannot be configured away.
Holds
Grandfathering is unavoidable. A market's hooks configuration is immutable with no setter, so no change of any kind reaches a deployed market. Accepting that was correct.
Holds
Replacement beats a bolt-on. A template carrying an explicit list is a strict superset of borrower-approval: an ordinary whitelist for ordinary borrowers, a lock at one lender when exclusivity is declared.
Change
Gate the deposit, not the approval. Policing credentials leaves the pull-provider loop and the sticky flag open. Refusing any depositor absent from the template's own list makes credentials irrelevant to the guarantee.
Change
Count admissions, never members. De-authorising does not remove tokens already held, so a lock keyed on current membership is defeated by de-authorise-then-authorise.
Change
Do not detect controller-ness. It is order-dependent and evadable by interposing a holder contract. Let the borrower declare exclusivity at creation and have the tranche factory verify it, checking template provenance rather than trusting version().
Change
Swap and check. Register additively, disable the old templates if the Foundation wants the default moved, and assert exclusivity at set creation. The swap makes the good configuration the default; the check is what makes it a guarantee.

Phase 0 is not an alternative to Phase 1. It ships first, it is the only tier that reaches markets that already exist, and it addresses the harm that exists today: senior's protection is thinner than the name implies and it also moves, and nothing in the current stack surfaces either fact.

Not fixed by any of this: protocol fees, which rank ahead of unprocessed lender claims, cannot be hooked, and are accepted as facility terms; a borrower running two exclusive markets against the same credit, which is a covenant question; and borrower conduct generally, since the borrower still sets APR, capacity, reserve ratio and grace, and still decides whether to repay. The tranche layer's answer to that last one is the waterfall and the wind-down trigger, not the access policy.
